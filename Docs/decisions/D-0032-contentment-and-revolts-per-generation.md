# D-0032: Contentment per generation; any generation can power a revolt

**Status:** Accepted (Norm, 2026-10-14); generation-specific consequences Proposed | **Date:** 2026-10-14 | **Area:** Simulation

## Context
Q-022: culture through trade (D-0025) compares each receiving cohort's own contentment with the supplier's adults'.

## Decision
Norm: "Separate contentment per generation is acceptable. Any given generation could power a revolt, potentially
with different consequences."
- Youth, adults and elders each have their own contentment. Claude's cheap form (Q-022 option b): each cohort's
  expressed vices (its own disposition plus the national mood) meet the nation's outlets. National contentment is
  the population-weighted average, so model v0 behavior carries over.
- Unrest is tracked per generation too, and any of the three can start a revolt.
- [PROPOSED] Consequences by generation:
  - Youth: the most frequent and most violent; the new type leans to the youth's own alignment, often a break
    with the past.
  - Adults: rarer and better organized; most likely to succeed; the new type follows the whole population (D-0024).
  - Elders: rare and mostly bloodless; lean toward restoring the previous or a traditional type (Monarchy, Theocracy).
  - The revolting generation's alignment weighs double when the new type is chosen.

## Because
Norm's call. Generational revolts give the cohort model visible stakes: a curriculum that frustrates the young can
topple a government that the elders still love.
