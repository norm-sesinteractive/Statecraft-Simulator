# The hidden hand

> **Owns:** the player's shadow organization: what drives it, how it loses, suspicion and exposure, false
> revolutions, and its reach across nations. **Budget:** 250 lines.
> **Read when:** designing anything about defeat, suspicion, the government as a shield, or expansion.

Norm's words are in [PITCH.md](PITCH.md) (session 2 sections). Decisions: D-0018 to D-0024. Everything here is the
accepted starting design (D-0023), open to tuning. Built in model v1 (2026-10-14): suspicion, the shield,
revolutions, votes, generational revolts and the overthrow of the hand; conquest, starvation and rivals come later.

## What drives it (D-0018)

No goals and no score. Norm: the organization is "simply driven by the desire of the player to manipulate the
population(s) of the game". The game ends only in defeat; the tension comes from keeping the river within its
banks while bending it to your designs.

## How a nation is lost (D-0019)

| Defeat | What happens | Planned |
|---|---|---|
| Military conquest | another country takes the nation by force | M3 |
| Cultural conquest | the population falls under another shadow organization, through trade-borne culture (D-0025) | M3 |
| Starvation | the people run out of resources and die | M2 |
| Overthrow of the hidden hand | a revolt goes past the government and topples the shadow organization | built (model v1) |

A revolt that topples only the government is survivable: that is what the government is for (D-0021). Losing the
home nation ends the game; nations gained later can be lost as setbacks (D-0023).

## The government as a shield (D-0021)

Norm: "The government is just a shield for the shadow organization of the player. They are another tool to absorb
or stoke the whims of the people and there to take the fall if the people become unruly." Government types and
how they change: [INSTITUTIONS.md](INSTITUTIONS.md), "Politics".

## Suspicion (D-0020)

Two tiers per nation: **government suspicion** (the rulers are not serving us) and **shadow suspicion** (someone
behind the rulers is pulling strings).

Raised by (Norm): "extremely high science", "frustrations with infrastructure", and vices or education "out of
whack". Concretely:
- An educated, curious population: high Science schooling and high Pride.
- Frustrated Envy and Greed where Infrastructure and Commerce outlets are thin (people see the system blocking them).
- Any vice far above or below human nature, or a curriculum or airtime mix concentrated on one thing.
- Abrupt, large lever swings (the river visibly changes course overnight).
- Long suffering with no visible cause.

Lowered by:
- Sloth (acceptance) and satisfied Gluttony ("bread and circuses").
- Neutral programming in the media mix.
- The government taking the fall: a revolution or vote that replaces the government vents most government suspicion.

How the tiers connect: government suspicion that stays high for years leaks into shadow suspicion. Revolutions vent
the government tier; each one that changes nothing for the people vents less and leaks more ("new masters, same
strings"). Shadow suspicion has no shield: past a breaking point, a revolt aims at the hidden hand itself.

Visibility: the player sees both tiers as fuzzy gauges, plus warning signs (pamphlets, investigations,
whistleblowers) as events, never exact numbers.

## Revolutions and false revolutions (D-0020, D-0021, D-0024)

Norm: "Perhaps you foment a false revolution to change government types and try to reduce suspicions."
- **Fomented by the player:** choose a target type and foment. It needs some unrest to work with, takes months,
  costs lives and stability, can fail, and vents government suspicion when it succeeds.
- **Voted (democracies):** the player or the people start a vote; cheaper and bloodless, but the people can vote no.
- **Revolts the people start:** the new government is "whatever government type the current population would be
  most aligned with, even if it's the same one they just revolted from" (D-0024).
- **Any generation can revolt** (D-0032): "Any given generation could power a revolt, potentially with different
  consequences." Accepted starting values: youth revolts are the most frequent and violent and lean to a break with the past;
  adult revolts are organized and likeliest to succeed; elder revolts are rare, mostly bloodless and lean to
  restoring a traditional type. The revolting generation's alignment weighs double in choosing the new type.

## Reach and rivals (D-0022)

The player starts with one home nation and gains others when one of their nations conquers another, militarily or
culturally. Rival shadow organizations steer other nations and can take the player's the same ways. In a gained
nation the player has the same levers; a culturally conquered nation keeps its government type until changed.
