# The hidden hand

> **Owns:** the player's shadow organization: what drives it, how it loses, suspicion and exposure, false
> revolutions, and its reach across nations. **Budget:** 250 lines.
> **Read when:** designing anything about defeat, suspicion, the government as a shield, or expansion.

Norm's words are in [PITCH.md](PITCH.md) (session 2 section). Decisions: D-0018 to D-0022. Proposed mechanics
are marked [PROPOSED] and are not built yet.

## What drives it (D-0018)

No goals and no score. Norm: the organization is "simply driven by the desire of the player to manipulate the
population(s) of the game". The game ends only in defeat; the tension comes from keeping the river within its
banks while bending it to your designs.

## How a nation is lost (D-0019)

| Defeat | What happens | Built? |
|---|---|---|
| Military conquest | another country takes the nation by force | [PLANNED M3] |
| Cultural conquest | the population falls under another shadow organization | [PLANNED M3] |
| Starvation | the people run out of resources and die | [PLANNED M2] |
| Overthrow of the hidden hand | a revolt goes past the government and topples the shadow organization | [PLANNED M1] |

A revolt that topples only the government is survivable: that is what the government is for (D-0021).
[PROPOSED] Losing the home nation ends the game; nations gained later can be lost as setbacks (Q-017).

## The government as a shield (D-0021)

Norm: "The government is just a shield for the shadow organization of the player. They are another tool to absorb
or stoke the whims of the people and there to take the fall if the people become unruly." Government types and
how they change: [INSTITUTIONS.md](INSTITUTIONS.md), "Politics".

## Suspicion (D-0020)

Two tiers per nation: **government suspicion** (the rulers are not serving us) and **shadow suspicion** (someone
behind the rulers is pulling strings).

Raised by (Norm): "extremely high science", "frustrations with infrastructure", and vices or education "out of whack".
[PROPOSED] concretely:
- An educated, curious population: high Science schooling and high Pride.
- Frustrated Envy and Greed where Infrastructure and Commerce outlets are thin (people see the system blocking them).
- Any vice far above or below human nature, or a curriculum or airtime mix concentrated on one thing.
- Abrupt, large lever swings (the river visibly changes course overnight).
- Long suffering with no visible cause.

Lowered by [PROPOSED]:
- Sloth (acceptance) and satisfied Gluttony ("bread and circuses").
- Neutral programming in the media mix.
- The government taking the fall: a revolution or vote that replaces the government vents most government suspicion.

How the tiers connect [PROPOSED]: government suspicion that stays high for years leaks into shadow suspicion.
Revolutions vent the government tier; each one that changes nothing for the people vents less and leaks more ("new
masters, same strings"). Shadow suspicion has no shield: once it passes a breaking point, a revolt aims at the
hidden hand itself.

Visibility [PROPOSED]: the player sees both tiers as fuzzy gauges, plus warning signs (pamphlets, investigations,
whistleblowers) as events, never exact numbers. Q-018.

## False revolutions (D-0020, D-0021)

Norm: "Perhaps you foment a false revolution to change government types and try to reduce suspicions."
[PROPOSED] The same action as changing the government type: choose a target type and foment. It needs some unrest
to work with, takes months, costs lives and stability, and vents government suspicion when it succeeds. A
democracy can hold a vote instead: cheaper and bloodless, but the people can vote no.

## Reach and rivals (D-0022)

The player starts with one home nation and gains others when one of their nations conquers another, militarily
or culturally. Rival shadow organizations steer other nations and can take the player's the same ways.
[PROPOSED] What the player controls in a gained nation is the same set of levers; a culturally conquered nation
keeps its own government type until the player changes it.
